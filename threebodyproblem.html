The Three-Body Problem

by Cixin Liu

Translated by Ken Liu

Quotes

  1. “She waved the battle banner as though brandishing her burning youth, trusting that the enemy would be burnt to ashes in the revolutionary flames, imagining that an ideal world would be born tomorrow from the ardor and zeal coursing through her blood. . . She was intoxicated by her brilliant, crimson dream until a bullet pierced her chest.” (10)
  2. “‘No, emptiness is not nothing. Emptiness is a type of existence.’” (192)
  3. “Ye felt this interminable wave was an abstract view of the universe: one end connected to the endless past, the other to the endless future, and in the middle only the ups and downs of random chance – without life, without pattern, the peaks and valleys at different heights like uneven grains of sand, the whole curve like a one-dimensional desert made all of the grains of sand lined up in a row, lonely, desolate, so long that it was intolerable. You could follow it and go forward or backward as long as you liked, but you’d never find the end.” (271)
  4. “Look at them, the bugs. Humans have used everything in their power to extinguish them, every kind of poison, aerial sprays, introducing and cultivating their natural predators, searching for and destroying their eggs, using genetic modification to sterilize them, burning with fire, drowning with water. Every family has bug spray, every desk has a fly-swatter under it. . . this long war has been going on for the entire history of human civilization. But the outcome is still in doubt. The bugs have not been eliminated. They still proudly live between the heavens and the earth, and their numbers have not diminished from the time before the appearance of the humans.” (387-388)

Thoughts

  • The story was interesting and engaging but the characters felt a bit flat to me
    • I still feel like I don’t really know who Wang is, and his wife is pretty much a nonentity
    • Ye felt a little more well realized, since we saw enough of her past to understand her feelings of loneliness and isolation and disillusionment with the world
  • It sucks that so many of the compelling female characters die… Yang Dong and Shen Yufei
  • The writing is serviceable with some truly beautiful moments
    • combining science and poetry in a way only a scientist can
  • I was a little concerned about the politics of the book – does Liu actually think that humanity is fundamentally evil and needs some superior power to save it?
    • But I don’t think that’s the point: Ye is a sympathetic character, but her feelings and actions are not necessarily framed as correct, just perhaps understandable after growing up during the Cultural Revolution
  • This book is about trying to make sense of humanity through science, and realizing that there is no sense to be made, at least not on a larger scale
    • The world, history, science, does not and cannot follow a predetermined path, and all predictions are imperfect. Yet we still keep going
    • We are nothing, we are bugs, infinitesimal specks in the vastness of the universe, but like bugs we have the tenacity to survive, to try to make sense of the chaos, despite the impossibility of the task. What else is there?
  • I’m sure using the the Cultural Revolution to frame the entire book was very intentional, as a time of unmitigated chaos which Chinese society is still trying to make sense of
    • There seems to be a parallel between factional fighting within the Red Guards and the factional fighting within the ETO, each side convinced of its righteousness, unable to see the harm they’re causing, unable to see how similar they are to their opponents
  • The Trisolarans too I think are not as far off from humanity as either the humans or the Trisolarans think
    • The narrative very intentionally uses similar language in the Trisolaran section to create a parallel between the listener and Ye, both isolated and disaffected
    • We are both sentient, living and flawed
    • Trisolaris cannot be earth’s savior, but it also will not be its destruction. It hasn’t achieved some higher state of morality because that’s not how progress works. Trisolarans are just as “human” as we are, in all the dimensions contained in that word
